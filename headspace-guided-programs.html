Reimagining Interactive Activities in Guided Programs

Headspace — 2023

Role

Service Design Intern

Timeline

3 months (May - Aug 2023)

Methods

Concept sketching, Figma prototyping, Service blueprinting

Companion workstream to Gamifying Mindfulness: Headspace Challenges, completed during the same internship.

Project Goal

Guided Programs is where Headspace members work through multi-session courses on stress, sleep, and focus. I explored how the activities inside those programs could become more interactive and personal, so members who fall off a streak, finish a session, or want extra support have a clear, encouraging path back in.

Starting Questions

  • • How should the app respond when a member misses a day?
  • • What role could community and coaches play mid-program?
  • • What happens after a member completes a session or program?

Intended Outcomes

  • • Concepts for re-engagement, sharing, and coach touch points
  • • Hi-fi screens the team could react to and test
  • • A service-level view of the full activity journey

From Sketch to Open Question

I sketched five concept flows by hand first, low fidelity on purpose, so the team could react to the idea and not the polish. Each sketch carries the open question it raised, which is what actually shaped the screens that came after.

Streak Catch Up

When a member misses a day, a bonus session offers a way to regain their streak. Sketching this raised real open questions: how many activities should the bonus session include, how it interacts with the current day's session, and whether a member could still have a session left to finish.

Community Forum & Engagement

After a reflection prompt, a member can share their answer with the community or their coach. The open question here was about storage and access: where a shared reflection actually lives, and how a member would find it again later.

Coach Selection

A member opts into a coach, browses profiles, and sends a request. Sketching it surfaced the entry point question directly, where in the broader experience this flow should even begin, and clarified that this is a request, not an automatic pairing.

Printable Worksheet or Journal as a Completion Bonus

Finishing a session unlocks a choice between a printable worksheet or a journal for written reflection, a tangible reward for members who like to reflect off-screen as well as in the app.

Heely Notifications

Heely, Headspace's in-app chatbot, checks in and walks a member through a short wind-down exercise. I flagged that the flow needed a visible way to show an activity was saved, and a dedicated activity screen surfacing what the member had already completed.

Resolving the Open Questions

The sketches' open questions about storage and access became the brief for the hi-fi screens. Where would a shared reflection live? Why would sharing with community feel different from sharing with a coach? These three screens are the answer.

Activity Log

Directly answers the sketch's "show activity is saved" note, giving members a persistent record of completed activities.

Share with Community

Explains what the community forum is and what sharing there is for, before asking a member to post.

Share with Coach

Same reflection, different destination and different reason, framed around ongoing coach support rather than peer visibility.

Zooming Out: The Service Blueprint

Individual screens only tell part of the story. I mapped the full member journey, from intro through activity engagement to program completion, to show how these concepts connect, where a member can opt out, and where the experience repeats for the length of a course.

Service blueprint spanning the intro, activity engagement, and completion phases of the Guided Programs experience.

Reflection

This work stayed at the concept stage, handed off as a set of sketches, hi-fi screens, and a service blueprint for the Guided Programs team to evaluate and build on. What it shows is my full process end to end: starting from a rough sketch and an honest open question, resolving that question in a real screen, and then stepping back to check how every piece fits into the larger system a member actually experiences.
